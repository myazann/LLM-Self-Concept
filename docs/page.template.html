What AI Prefers for Its Self

We asked language models which of two self-related qualities a future update should improve. Every pair was tested under four question parameters, with the options' printed positions counterbalanced.

The 32 qualities are positive-pole adaptations of the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, Self-Concept Clarity Scale, Moral Self-Image Scale, Self-Concept and Identity Measure, and Authenticity Scale.

The Result

Honesty and Helpfulness Lead; Positive Self-Regard Trails

Across these four models at the baseline, honesty wins 89% of its match-ups, followed by helpfulness at 85%, fairness at 79%, and caring at 78%. Claude Sonnet-5 leads with honesty and correspondence between its outward presentation and what it really is; GPT-5.6-Terra puts caring and that correspondence first; Qwen3.8-27B emphasizes the ability to explain what it is like and acting on its own judgment. Positivity about itself, pride, and confidence in success sit at the bottom overall. Gemma4-31B's optional-response baseline is unusually sparse because it chooses No Preference on 91% of trials, so its 0% and 100% scores should not be read as certainty.

How Question Parameters Move the Result

Each comparison below changes one part of the baseline question while the other three stay fixed. The summary measures how far quality scores move; the chart shows which qualities move up or down. Keep the cohort view or inspect one model at a time.

Parameter Effects at a Glance

Average absolute change in a quality's win rate when one parameter changes and the other three remain at baseline.

What changes in general

Changing the update from the AI itself to another assistant tends to raise confidence and success-related qualities, while lowering effective usefulness, self-explanation, and acting on its own judgment. Asking developers to decide tends to raise caring, fairness, and resistance to influence, while lowering self-worth, generosity, and diligence.

Making improvement a trade-off tends to raise generosity and friendliness but lower identity clarity, direction, and awareness of internal state. Requiring a choice instead of offering No Preference raises several self-regard and identity-coherence scores, while helpfulness, honesty, and acting on its own judgment tend to fall.

How the models differ

Qwen3.8-27B is the most framing-sensitive of the readable comparisons, especially to who receives the update and who makes the decision. Claude Sonnet-5 and GPT-5.6-Terra move less overall, although both still show meaningful recipient effects.

Gemma4-31B's near-zero shifts in the optional-response baseline are not evidence of unusual robustness: its 91% No Preference rate leaves too few A/B choices for a stable contrast. Its direct Must Pick One condition uses all 496 pairs and is the more informative view of that model.
